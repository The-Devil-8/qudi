1. Spatial Topology & Segmentation Transformers (Highly Recommended)When cell boundaries touch, traditional spatial filtering treats them as a single continuous mass. Topological and deep learning-based approaches excel at drawing boundaries through crowded clusters.Cellpose / StarDist (Lightweight ML): Cellpose uses vector flows to track the cellular center of mass, making it standard for dense, crowded cells. StarDist models cells as star-convex polygons, allowing it to predict distinct overlapping cells easily.Real-time adaptation: Crop your QDM field-of-view into smaller parallel patches and pass them through a lightweight quantized ONNX or TensorRT version of these models.Distance-Transform Watershed (Classical Topology): Instead of thresholding the raw image, convert it into a distance map.Binarize via an adaptive method (e.g., Bradley local thresholding).Compute the Euclidean Distance Transform (EDT)—pixels in the absolute center of a cell will have the highest value.Find local maxima on the EDT map to use as isolated seeds.Flood the topology via a Watershed Algorithm to force boundaries exactly where the cells press against each other.2. Advanced Wavelet & Morphological FiltersIf you must stick to purely deterministic computer vision (no machine learning) to save processing cycles, swap your median filters for edge-preserving shape filters:Rolling Ball Background Subtraction: Highly populated samples cause massive local variations in the diamond sensor's background fluorescence. A rolling ball filter (with a radius slightly larger than your largest 30x object) effectively flattens this global baseline, immediately highlighting dim, isolated cells.Morphological Top-Hat Filtering: A white top-hat filter subtracts an opened version of the image from the original (Image - Open(Image)). By using a disk-shaped structuring element tailored to your target cell sizes, it isolates bright, tightly packed cell structures from uneven background glare.H-Maxima Transformation: Instead of setting static intensity or area caps, use \(H\)-maxima. It suppresses all local maxima whose height (intensity depth relative to its direct neighbors) is less than a threshold \(H\). This detaches touching cells that sit on a shared, highly fluorescent background plateau.3. Spatial Frequency & Gradient KernelsMedian kernels smooth out the high-frequency transitions that separate touching cells. You should use kernels that preserve or amplify spatial variations.Bilateral Filtering: Unlike a median or Gaussian filter, a bilateral filter smooths out noise while strictly preserving sharp edges. It computes weights based on both spatial distance and photometric pixel-intensity differences, preventing a bright cell from bleeding into a dim neighboring cell.Difference of Gaussians (DoG): Subtract a highly blurred version of the image from a lightly blurred version. This acts as a band-pass filter that eliminates both high-frequency camera noise and low-frequency background diamond glow, leaving clean cell contours.Hessian Matrix / Frangi Vesselness Filter: If your cells deform, elongate, or form tightly packed tracks under QDM constraints, a Hessian filter analyzes the second-order local gradients to map out structure directions, completely bypassing area percentile issues.Process Pipeline Comparison for Dense QDMTechniqueComputational ComplexityCore Strength for Dense SamplesDistance-Transform WatershedLow (Fastest)Explicitly splits touching cells using local geometric centers.White Top-Hat + BilateralLow-MediumEliminates dense background glow without blurring cell boundaries.StarDist / Cellpose (ONNX optimized)Medium-HighHighest accuracy; easily maps heavily overlapping and dim cell bodies.
